# Proportional review checklist

Use only applicable checks. A short feature does not need a paragraph for every item.

- Outcome, actor, trigger and scope are explicit.
- Eligibility, permissions and approval boundaries are defined.
- Mandatory inputs, limits and invalid combinations are specified.
- Financial effect dates differ clearly from processing or recording dates.
- Backdating, future dates and calendar rules are resolved where applicable.
- Calculations, precision, rounding and units are unambiguous.
- Effects on balances, accruals, schedules, arrears and loan status are explicit.
- Configuration scope, defaults and applicability to existing loans are resolved where relevant.
- Failure, duplicate submission, correction and reversal behaviour are addressed or explicitly excluded with dependencies.
- Outputs and downstream observable effects are defined where applicable.
- Evidence has repository/version references; conflicts and gaps remain visible.
- Confirmed decisions support normative rules. No recommendation has become a rule by accident.
- Examples cover relevant normal, boundary and failure cases without duplicating rules.
- IDs and cross-references are correct. Two readers can derive the same expected outcome.
- Readiness and acceptance are explicit; unresolved material questions block readiness for their scope.

- Record structure fits the change; no empty files are required solely by a template. Each substantive fact has an authoritative location.
- Where financial calculations are in scope, readiness requires independently checked numerical examples sufficient to establish expected results. Cover a complete scenario and relevant boundaries or exceptions in proportion to the change. Record inputs, dates, calculation conventions, expected amounts and checking method. A reviewed manual calculation, separate spreadsheet or independently derived calculation can provide the check; expected values generated by the same implementation under test are not independent validation. Record outstanding checks honestly; this guidance does not validate existing examples.
